Most of us think about what we put on our skin and hair. Far fewer of us think about what we wash it in. A ten-minute shower can expose your skin and scalp to chlorine, trace metals and mineral scale, and the effects add up quietly over months.

What’s in the water

Municipal water is disinfected with chlorine or chloramine so it’s safe to drink. In warm, steamy air, those compounds vaporise, and open pores and a hot scalp absorb more than you’d expect. Hard water adds mineral buildup that leaves hair rough and skin tight.

Better skin and hair don’t just start with products. They start with water.

What a filter changes

A shower filter sits between the pipe and the showerhead. Media such as KDF-55 and calcium sulphite convert and trap chlorine and metals before the water reaches you. Most people notice softer hair first, then calmer skin over several weeks.

Making it a ritual

Keep the water warm rather than hot, rinse for a final thirty seconds on cool, and replace the cartridge about every six months.
